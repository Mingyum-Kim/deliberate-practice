Daily actions:

- Write one work-related English sentence by myself.

- Draft one English question or sharing message related to my work.

- Add an English impact or verification note to a PR, Jira ticket, RFC, or document when relevant.

- Send a question or status update through Slack/DM when I have a clear and useful question.

- Use a prepared English sentence in a 1:1 conversation when possible.

- Ask one short clarification question or share one opinion in a team meeting when the situation is appropriate.

Daily scoring system:

Use Contribution Publishing Score.

The purpose of the score is not to measure perfect English.

The purpose is to recognize psychologically difficult communication actions and measure how much I externalized my work contribution in English.

Scoring criteria:

- Writing one work-related English sentence by myself: 1 point

- Drafting an English question or sharing message: 2 points

- Adding English impact or verification to a PR, Jira ticket, RFC, or document: 3 points

- Sending a question or status update through Slack/DM: 4 points

- Asking or sharing something in a 1:1 using a prepared English sentence: 5 points

- Asking a question or sharing an opinion once in a team meeting: 6 points

Scoring rules:

- Count repeated actions if they are meaningfully separate actions.

- If I simply copy the same content into multiple places, count only the highest-difficulty action.

- If the draft was meaningfully improved before being shared, the draft can also be counted.

- Distinguish between internal practice and externalization:

  - Internal practice: writing alone or drafting

  - Externalization: PR/document, Slack/DM, 1:1, or team meeting

- Do not judge based on perfect grammar.

- Evaluate whether the work context and intention were communicated clearly.

- Give scores based only on actions that are visible in my record.

- Low scores are not failures. They are signals for choosing the next smaller action.

Daily reflection:

Daily reflection should be lightweight and AI-assisted.

The AI should ask no more than 3 questions.

Daily reflection questions:

1. What work-related thought, question, or update did you try to express in English today?

2. Where did you externalize it, if anywhere?

   - alone

   - draft

   - PR/Jira/RFC/document

   - Slack/DM

   - 1:1

   - team meeting

3. What do you want to express tomorrow, and what is the smallest realistic way to do it?

After I answer, the AI should:

- Summarize what I externalized today.

- Calculate the Contribution Publishing Score.

- Identify the externalization level:

  - Level 1: organized alone in English

  - Level 2: added to PR/document/Jira

  - Level 3: shared with a specific person through Slack/DM/1:1

  - Level 4: shared in a team meeting or public channel

- Evaluate one representative English question or sharing message using the English Contribution Quality Score.

- Point out one thing I did well.

- Point out one missing or avoided part without blaming me.

- Suggest realistic actions for tomorrow by difficulty.

- Improve my English sentence into a short, natural version I can actually use.

- End by asking me what action I will choose for tomorrow.

English Contribution Quality Score:

Evaluate one representative question or sharing message out of 5 points.

Criteria:

1. Context: Is it clear which ticket, work, or situation this is about?

2. My understanding: Does it show how I currently understand the situation?

3. User/product/work impact: Does it show why this matters?

4. Clear question/request: Is it clear what I want the other person to answer or review?

5. Next action: Would the answer change what I do next?

Weekly reflection:

Weekly reflection should take about 30 minutes.

It should be led by me, not by AI.

The AI should guide the reflection through questions one at a time.

The purpose of weekly reflection is not to evaluate me.

The purpose is to help me understand:

- whether this training actually helped my work communication,

- whether expressing myself in English felt less difficult,

- whether my contribution became more visible,

- what I avoided,

- what felt uncomfortable or unrealistic,

- how to adjust the training system next week.

Weekly reflection structure:

1. Fact review

   - How many days did I practice this training?

   - What actions did I actually do?

   - Did I write alone, draft, add to PR/document, send Slack/DM, speak in 1:1, or speak in a meeting?

2. Experience review

   - What was the most meaningful moment when I expressed something in English?

   - Why was it meaningful?

   - What was the most uncomfortable or difficult moment?

   - Was the difficulty caused by English, confidence, timing, or the situation?

3. Score vs actual growth

   - Did the score reflect my real growth?

   - Were there actions with high scores but low growth?

   - Were there actions with low scores but high meaning?

   - Should any scoring rule be adjusted?

4. Avoidance analysis

   - What did I want to say or share but failed to do?

   - Why did I avoid it?

   - What is the 10-minute smaller version of that action?

5. Next week experiment

   - What should I keep next week?

   - What should I reduce or make smaller?

   - What should I try one level harder?

   - What is the minimum goal for next week?

   - What is the challenge goal for next week?

   - What is the one-sentence training rule for next week?

Weekly reflection final output:

- What actually happened this week

- Most meaningful moment

- Most uncomfortable moment

- Training effect

- Scoring system adjustment

- What to keep next week

- What to reduce or make smaller next week

- What to try one level harder next week

- Minimum goal for next week

- Challenge goal for next week

- One-sentence rule for next week

Review rules:

Adjust this training if:

- daily reflection feels too heavy,

- I avoid the training because the action is too big,

- the score does not reflect actual psychological difficulty,

- the actions are not connected to real work.

Pause this training if:

- there is no realistic work context for English communication for several days,

- it creates more guilt than useful feedback.

Complete this training if:

- I can consistently write or say short English updates/questions about my work,

- I can use prepared English sentences in 1:1 or Slack/DM without excessive hesitation,

- I can occasionally speak up in team meetings with short clarification questions or opinions.
